There are marathons you run for time. There are marathons you run to prove something to yourself. And then there are marathons you run because the course passes through one of the natural wonders of the world, and you know that no personal record will ever quite match what your eyes are about to see.

The Victoria Falls Marathon is that kind of race.

This year, a group of Glen Striders made the journey from Harare to Victoria Falls, and what we brought back was more than a finisher's medal.

---

## The Race

The Victoria Falls Marathon has been a fixture on the Zimbabwean running calendar for decades. Held each year in July, it draws runners from across Zimbabwe, the region, and the world — all drawn by the same irresistible combination: a challenging course, spectacular scenery, and the roar of the falls carried on the morning air.

The event offers a full marathon (42.2km), a half marathon (21.1km), and a fun run, making it accessible to runners at every level. Our group spread across all three distances, which meant that whatever the day held, we would all be out on the road together — in spirit, if not always in step.

## The Morning of the Race

We were up before dawn. The air in Victoria Falls in July carries a particular quality — cool and sharp, with a faint mist from the falls already drifting over the town as the first light arrived. Standing at the start line, surrounded by hundreds of runners from across the continent, there was a feeling that something significant was about to happen.

It always feels that way at the start of a race. But here, with the sound of the Zambezi somewhere in the near distance and the sky beginning to colour above the gorge, that feeling had an extra dimension.

The gun went off. We went with it.

---

## The Course

The Victoria Falls Marathon course is beautiful and, at times, deceptively demanding. Runners wind through the town, past the legendary Victoria Falls Hotel, and along roads that trace the edge of the gorge. On a clear morning, the spray from the falls rises above the treeline like smoke from a distant fire.

Wildlife sightings are not uncommon. Warthogs, baboons, and the occasional elephant have all been spotted on or near the course over the years. Our group encountered a family of warthogs trotting along the roadside just after the 15km mark — which provided an unexpected motivation boost for those of us who were beginning to feel the road.

The second half of the marathon is where the race truly begins. The kilometres feel longer. The legs begin to negotiate terms. But the finish line at the Victoria Falls Rainforest is among the most beautiful places on earth to cross a timing mat, and that knowledge carries you.

---

## Our Striders

Every one of our runners who toed the start line crossed the finish line. That is the detail that matters most.

Some of our group were running the distance for the first time — stepping up from the half marathon to a full 42.2km, carrying months of early morning preparation with them. Others were chasing personal bests. A few were simply there for the experience, the stories, and the shared miles.

Whatever brought each of us to the start, the same thing brought us through: the knowledge that we were not running alone.

> "When I hit the wall at kilometre 34, I thought about every Saturday long run we did together. About getting up at 4am when I didn't want to. About the club. That's what got me to the finish."

That is what a running community is for. Not the training plans or the gear recommendations or the group chats — though those help. It is the weight of shared effort, accumulated over hundreds of mornings, that you carry with you when the race gets hard.

---

## Beyond the Race

Victoria Falls is a place worth slowing down for. In the days around the race, our group visited the falls themselves — standing at the lip of the gorge, feeling the spray on your face, watching the Zambezi fall away into the chasm below. It is one of those places that reminds you of the scale of the world and your small, grateful place within it.

We shared meals together, relived the race in exhaustive and contradictory detail, and laughed a great deal. Post-race meals taste better than any other meals. Everything tastes better when your legs have earned it.

---

## What the Vic Falls Marathon Gives You

Every runner takes something different from this race. A first marathon finish. A new personal best. A photograph taken at the edge of the gorge. The memory of running through African dawn with hundreds of others who got up early, showed up, and put one foot in front of the other until the finish line arrived.

What it gave our group was something that is harder to name: a shared reference point that will come up for years. *"Remember Vic Falls?"* will appear in conversations on early morning training runs, and everyone who was there will know exactly what it means.

It means: we did something extraordinary. Together.
